One application of using the convective object database was understanding how frequent convective cores and storm systems occur. Convective storm systems most frequently occur over the tropics, particularly over land, and have the largest updraft intensities over the Congo Basin in Africa. We found that convective cores frequently occur over the West Pacific and Indian Oceans, which is where rainfall accumulation is the highest and where storm systems are the largest. A theory proposed in the 1970s using little observational data estimated that 1600-2400 convective updrafts that reach the tropopause occur at any time within the tropics. We elaborated on this theory using a combination of observations from multiple satellites to provide an estimate of 800-1700 convective updrafts occurring at any given time. By documenting this estimate, we provided a potential limit related to the amount of energy that is transported into the upper atmosphere by convective updrafts.
